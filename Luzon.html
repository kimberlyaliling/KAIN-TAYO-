The northern island group of the Philippines

Luzon dishes

Luzon sits at the northern end of the Philippines, and is the country’s largest and most populous island. It’s known for its mountains, beaches and coral reefs, and is home to Manila, the national capital. Set on a deep bay with famed sunsets, the city has many Spanish-colonial landmarks, national memorials and monuments, a centuries-old Chinatown and a diversity of museums.

Luzon Cuisine

Luzon cuisine features distinct regional flavors ranging from the salty, vegetable-heavy dishes of the North to the spicy, coconut-based specialties of the South.

Bicol Express

A spicy and flavorful dish from the Bicol region, featuring tender meat in a rich, aromatic sauce.

Ilocos Norte

Ladek

A traditional Ilocano dish made with pork belly, often served with a savory sauce.

Bicol

Laing

Laing is a popular Filipino dish made from shredded or whole taro leaves cooked in rich coconut milk, chili peppers, and aromatics.

Central Luzon

Sisig

Sisig is a popular Ilocano dish made from chopped pork face and liver, seasoned with calamansi and served with rice.

Ilocos Norte

Pinakbet

Pinakbet is a popular Ilocano dish made from a variety of vegetables cooked in a savory sauce.

Southern Luzon

Bulalo

Bulalo is a hearty beef soup from Southern Luzon, known for its rich and flavorful broth.

Southern/Eastern Luzon

Pancit Habhab

Pancit Habhab is a popular Filipino noodle dish made with rice noodles, vegetables, and a savory sauce.

Southern Luzon

Lomi

Lomi (or Pancit Lomi) is a famous Filipino comfort food made with thick, fresh egg noodles soaked in a rich, savory broth thickened with starch and beaten eggs.

Central Luzon

Kare-Kare

Kare-Kare is a popular Filipino stew made with oxtail, vegetables, and a rich, savory sauce.
