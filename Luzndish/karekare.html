Pampanga

Kare-Kare

Kare-Kare is a rich, festive Filipino peanut stew widely associated with Pampanga and the broader Tagalog region in Central and Southern Luzon. It is traditionally made with slow-cooked oxtail and beef tripe, submerged in a thick, savory peanut sauce naturally colored bright orange with annatto (atsuete). The stew features a medley of indigenous vegetables, including banana blossom (puso ng saging), eggplant, string beans, and pechay. Because the peanut sauce itself is mild and slightly sweet, Kare-Kare is strictly eaten with a side of salty, savory sautéed shrimp paste (bagoong alamang) to complete its iconic flavor profile.

Gather

Ingredients

    For the Meat and Broth

  • Oxtail, cut into serving slices
  • Beef tripe (tuwalya), cleaned and sliced
  • Water (for boiling)
  • Onion, quartered

    • For the Peanut Sauce

  • Peanut butter (unsweetened preferred) or ground roasted peanuts
  • Toasted rice flour (for thickening)
  • Annatto seeds (atsuete) soaked in warm water to extract color
  • Garlic, minced
  • Onion, chopped
  • Cooking oil

    • For the Vegetables

  • Banana blossom (puso ng saging), sliced and soaked in salted water
  • Eggplant, sliced diagonally
  • String beans (sitaw), cut into 2-inch lengths
  • Pechay (bok choy)

    • For Serving

  • Sautéed shrimp paste (bagoong alamang)
  • Steamed white rice

Make it

Recipe

  1. In a large heavy pot or pressure cooker, place the oxtail, beef tripe, and quartered onion. Cover with water and boil until the meats are completely tender (about 2.5 to 3 hours on a stovetop, or 45 minutes in a pressure cooker). Skim the scum off the top.
  2. Once tender, remove the meat and strain the beef broth. Set both aside.
  3. In a separate pan, blanch the string beans, eggplant, banana blossom, and pechay in boiling water for a few minutes. Drain and set aside so they don't overcook in the stew.
  4. In a large pot, heat oil and sauté the minced garlic and chopped onion until aromatic and translucent.
  5. Add the tender oxtail and tripe, stirring them in the aromatics for a couple of minutes.
  6. Pour in about 4 to 5 cups of the reserved beef broth and bring to a simmer.
  7. Stir in the peanut butter (or ground peanuts) and the red annatto water (straining out the seeds). Mix well until the peanut butter dissolves completely and the broth turns a rich orange.
  8. Gradually whisk in the toasted rice flour (dissolved in a little water first to prevent lumps). Let it simmer for another 5 to 10 minutes, stirring frequently, until the sauce thickens to your desired consistency.
  9. You can either mix the blanched vegetables directly into the pot, or arrange them nicely on top when transferring to a serving dish.
  10. Serve hot alongside steaming white rice and a small saucer of sautéed shrimp paste (bagoong alamang) for dipping.
