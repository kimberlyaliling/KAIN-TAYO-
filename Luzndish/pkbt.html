Ilocos Norte

Pinakbet

Pinakbet is a popular vegetable dish that originated in the Ilocos Region of Northern Luzon. It is traditionally made with mixed indigenous vegetables like bitter melon (ampalaya), eggplant, tomatoes, okra, and string beans, all cooked in a savory fermented fish paste (bagoong isda). The Ilocano version is known for its distinct savory-bitter flavor profile and often includes crispy pork belly or bagnet to add richness. Unlike other stir-fried vegetable dishes, authentic Ilocano pinakbet is cooked by simmering the vegetables in their own juices and the bagoong rather than using a lot of water.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Pork belly (sliced) or bagnet
  • Bitter melon (ampalaya), cored and sliced
  • Eggplant, sliced
  • Okra
  • String beans (sitaw), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • Tomatoes, chopped
  • Ginger, sliced
  • Onion, chopped
  • Garlic, minced
  • Fermented fish paste (bagoong isda)
  • Water, as needed
  • Cooking oil

Make it

Recipe

  1. Heat oil in a pan or pot and sauté the garlic, onion, and ginger until aromatic.
  2. Add the sliced pork belly and cook until browned and tender. If using bagnet, you can add it towards the end to keep it crispy.
  3. Stir in the chopped tomatoes and cook until they soften and release their juices.
  4. Add the fermented fish paste (bagoong isda) and cook for another minute.
  5. Add a small amount of water and bring to a boil.
  6. Layer the vegetables based on their cooking time: start with the string beans, cooking for a few minutes.
  7. Add the bitter melon, eggplant, and okra. Cover the pot and let the vegetables steam and simmer until tender but not mushy.
  8. Gently toss the vegetables to coat them evenly with the sauce. Avoid over-stirring to prevent the squash from turning into mush.
  9. Serve hot with steamed rice.
