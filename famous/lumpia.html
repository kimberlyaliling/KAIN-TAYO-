Lumpiang Shanghai

Despite its name pointing to Chinese origins, Lumpiang Shanghai is a distinctly Filipino party staple eaten universally across the country. It is a type of egg roll filled with a savory mixture of ground pork, minced carrots, onions, and garlic, rolled tightly into thin crepe wrappers, and deep-fried until golden and crunchy. While often seen as an appetizer or party food, it is widely eaten as a daily ulam paired with rice and sweet chili sauce.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Ground pork (or a mix of pork and beef)
  • Lumpia wrappers (spring roll wrappers)
  • Carrots, finely minced
  • Onion, finely minced
  • Garlic, finely minced
  • Kinilaw (parsley or kinchay), finely chopped (optional)
  • Egg
  • Soy sauce, salt, and pepper
  • Cooking oil (for deep frying)
  • Sweet chili sauce (for dipping)

Make it

Recipe

  1. In a large mixing bowl, combine the ground pork, minced carrots, onion, garlic, kinchay, and raw egg.
  2. Season the mixture with soy sauce, salt, and black pepper. Mix thoroughly until well combined. (Tip: fry a tiny pinch of the mixture first to check the seasoning).
  3. Lay a lumpia wrapper flat. Place about a tablespoon of the meat mixture in a thin line near the bottom edge.
  4. Fold the bottom over the meat, fold in the sides, and roll it up tightly into a thin cylinder. Seal the top edge with a dab of water.
  5. Heat oil in a pan over medium heat..
  6. Deep-fry the rolls in batches for 5 to 7 minutes, turning them until they are golden brown and crispy on all sides.
  7. Drain on paper towels. Serve hot with rice and sweet chili sauce.
