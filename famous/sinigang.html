Sinigang

Sinigang is the ultimate Filipino comfort food, representing the nation's profound love for sour flavors. While the dish is eaten nationwide, its origins trace back to the Tagalog region. This sour soup uses a tamarind base (sampalok) to create a sharp, refreshing broth that cuts through the richness of the pork belly. It is loaded with local vegetables like water spinach (kangkong), radish, taro (gabi), and string beans.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Pork belly (liempo), cut into cubes
  • Fresh tamarind (sampalok) or tamarind soup mix
  • Taro (gabi), peeled and quartered (to thicken the soup)
  • White radish (labanos), sliced
  • String beans (sitaw), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • Eggplant, sliced diagonally
  • Tomatoes, quartered
  • Onion, quartered
  • Water spinach (kangkong)
  • Green finger chilies (siling haba)
  • Fish sauce (patis)
  • Water

Make it

Recipe

  1. In a large pot, bring water to a boil. Add the pork belly, onions, and tomatoes. Lower the heat and simmer for about 45 minutes until the pork begins to tenderize. Skim off any scum.
  2. Add the taro (gabi) and simmer until it begins to soften and slightly disintegrate, thickening the broth.
  3. If using fresh tamarind, boil it separately, mash the fruit, and strain the sour juice into the main pot. If using a mix, stir the powder in now.
  4. Add the white radish, eggplant, and string beans. Cook for 5 to 7 minutes.
  5. Season the soup with fish sauce to taste, and drop in the green finger chilies.
  6. Turn off the heat and submerge the water spinach (kangkong). Let the residual heat cook the leaves.
  7. Serve immediately with rice and a side of fish sauce with crushed chilies.
