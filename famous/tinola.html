Tinolang Manok

Tinolang Manok is a comforting, light, and ginger-heavy chicken soup deeply ingrained in Filipino culture (even referenced in Jose Rizal's novel, Noli Me Tangere). Eaten nationwide, this soothing broth is made by sautéing chicken in ginger, garlic, and onions, then boiling it with green papaya wedges and chili leaves. It is a staple household dish, especially popular during the rainy season or when someone is feeling under the weather.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Chicken, cut into serving pieces (native chicken is traditional)
  • Green papaya or chayote (sayote), cut into wedges
  • Chili leaves (dahon ng sili) or malunggay leaves
  • Ginger, julienned or sliced thickly
  • Garlic, minced
  • Onion, chopped
  • Fish sauce (patis)
  • Rice washing (hugas bigas) or water
  • Cooking oil

Make it

Recipe

  1. Heat oil in a pot and sauté the ginger, garlic, and onion until aromatic.
  2. Add the chicken pieces and cook until the outer skin turns slightly brown.
  3. Season with a few tablespoons of fish sauce and let it cook for another 2 minutes to absorb the flavor.
  4. Pour in the rice washing (or water) and bring to a rolling boil. Skim off any scum.
  5. Lower the heat, cover, and simmer until the chicken is tender (about 30-45 minutes depending on the chicken).
  6. Add the green papaya or chayote and cook for 5 to 7 minutes until tender.
  7. Turn off the heat and stir in the chili or malunggay leaves, allowing the residual heat to cook them instantly.
  8. Serve hot.
