Tortang Talong

Tortang Talong is a quintessential, everyday Filipino ulam. Eaten everywhere from humble provincial homes to modern city apartments, this simple dish involves roasting a whole eggplant until soft, peeling off the charred skin while keeping the stem intact, flattening the flesh, dipping it in a beaten egg mixture, and pan-frying it. It yields a dish that is crispy on the edges, creamy inside, and carries a distinct smoky flavor. It is almost exclusively paired with banana ketchup.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Long Chinese eggplants (talong)
  • Eggs (1 egg per 1 to 2 eggplants)
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Cooking oil
  • Banana ketchup (for serving)

Make it

Recipe

  1. Wash the eggplants but do not remove the stems. Pierce them a few times with a fork.
  2. Roast the eggplants directly over a gas stove flame or grill until the skin is completely charred and blackened, and the flesh inside is very soft.
  3. Let them cool slightly. Carefully peel off the charred skin using your fingers, leaving the stem attached.
  4. Place the peeled eggplant on a flat plate and gently mash/flatten the flesh using a fork.
  5. In a wide bowl, beat the eggs and season generously with salt and pepper.
  6. Submerge the flattened eggplant into the beaten egg, ensuring it is well-coated.
  7. Heat oil in a wide pan. Slide the egg-coated eggplant into the pan. Pour a little extra egg mixture over the top.
  8. Fry until golden brown on one side, then flip by holding the stem and using a spatula to fry the other side.
  9. Serve hot with rice and banana ketchup.
