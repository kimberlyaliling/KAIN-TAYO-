Chicken Piyanggang

Also hailing from the Tausug people of Sulu, Chicken Piyanggang is a distinctive chicken dish that shares the same foundational ingredient as Tiyula Itum: the burnt coconut paste (pamapa itum). However, instead of a soup, Piyanggang is a dry dish where the chicken is first braised in coconut milk and the black spice paste until tender, and then grilled over hot coals to impart a deep, smoky flavor.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Chicken, cut into serving pieces
  • Pamapa itum (burnt coconut paste mixed with garlic, ginger, turmeric, and chilies)
  • Coconut milk (gata)
  • Lemongrass, pounded
  • Onions, chopped
  • Salt and pepper
  • Cooking oil

Make it

Recipe

  1. Prepare the pamapa itum by roasting grated coconut until black and pounding it with the spices (garlic, ginger, turmeric, chilies).
  2. Rub the chicken pieces generously with half of the pamapa itum paste and marinate for at least an hour.
  3. In a wide pan or wok, sauté the chopped onions, then add the marinated chicken. Cook until the outside of the chicken changes color.
  4. Pour in the coconut milk and add the pounded lemongrass. Stir in the remaining pamapa itum.
  5. Simmer over low-medium heat until the chicken is cooked through and the coconut milk has reduced to a thick, oily, dark sauce that coats the meat.
  6. Remove the chicken pieces from the pan.
  7. Grill the chicken over hot coals just until slightly charred and smoky, basting it with the remaining thick sauce from the pan.
  8. Serve immediately with rice, spooning any leftover sauce over it.
