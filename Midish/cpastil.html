Pastil

Pastil (or Pater) is a popular, budget-friendly packed rice dish originating from the Maguindanao people. It is the ultimate Mindanaoan comfort food on-the-go. It consists of steamed rice topped with kagikit—finely shredded, savory, and mildly spicy cooked chicken, beef, or fish. The entire meal is tightly wrapped in a wilted banana leaf, which infuses the rice with a wonderful, earthy aroma.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Cooked white rice
  • Chicken breast, beef, or fish (boiled and finely shredded)
  • Garlic, minced
  • Onion, finely chopped
  • Soy sauce
  • Vinegar or calamansi (just a splash)
  • Black pepper and salt
  • Cooking oil
  • Banana leaves, wilted over an open flame

Make it

Recipe

  1. Boil the meat (chicken breast is most common) until completely cooked. Drain, let cool, and shred it very finely using your hands or a fork.
  2. Heat oil in a pan and sauté the garlic and onion until aromatic.
  3. Add the shredded meat and stir-fry for a few minutes.
  4. Season with soy sauce, a splash of vinegar or calamansi, black pepper, and salt.
  5. Continue to cook over low heat until the meat is dry, toasted, and very flavorful (this is the kagikit). Set aside.
  6. Prepare the wilted banana leaves by cutting them into squares.
  7. Place a scoop of warm, cooked rice in the center of a banana leaf.
  8. Top the rice generously with the kagikit.
  9. Fold the banana leaf over the rice to form a tight, neat rectangular packet.
  10. The Pastil is now ready to be eaten immediately or packed for a journey.
