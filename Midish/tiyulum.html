Tiyula Itlum

Tiyula Itum (literally translating to "Black Soup") is a celebrated royal dish of the Tausug people from the Sulu Archipelago. Often served during weddings and special occasions, it is a beef or goat soup defined by its striking pitch-black broth. This color and its unique, roasted, earthy flavor come from pamapa itum, a spice paste heavily reliant on burnt coconut meat. It also features a warm, spicy kick from ginger, turmeric, and chilies.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Beef chunks (with bone and marrow preferred)
  • Burnt coconut meat (niyog), pounded into a paste
  • Lemongrass (tanglad), pounded
  • Turmeric (luyang dilaw), pounded
  • Ginger and garlic, pounded
  • Galangal (langkawas), optional but traditional
  • Bird's eye chilies (siling labuyo)
  • Salt or fish sauce to taste
  • Cooking oil and water

Make it

Recipe

  1. To make the pamapa itum (burnt coconut paste), roast grated mature coconut meat in a dry pan until it turns completely black (but not completely ashed). Pound it in a mortar and pestle along with garlic, ginger, turmeric, and chilies until it forms a dark, oily paste.
  2. In a heavy pot, massage the beef chunks with a generous amount of the pamapa itum paste. Let it marinate for about 30 minutes.
  3. Place the pot over medium heat and sauté the beef in its marinade until the meat releases its juices and lightly browns.
  4. Add the pounded lemongrass and pour enough water to submerge the beef.
  5. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat to a simmer. Cover and cook for 2 to 3 hours until the beef is meltingly tender.
  6. Season with salt or fish sauce, and add more chilies if you prefer it spicier.
  7. Serve piping hot with rice.
