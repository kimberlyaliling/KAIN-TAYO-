Curacha with Alavar sauce

Curacha is a unique crustacean—a hybrid-looking spanner crab (often called a red frog crab)—found primarily in the waters of Sulu and Zamboanga. It is famously served steamed and then smothered in Alavar Sauce, a legendary, secret-recipe sauce originating from Alavar Seafood Restaurant in Zamboanga City. The sauce is a rich, creamy, and mildly spicy blend of coconut milk, aligue (crab fat), and various Mindanaoan spices.

Gather

Ingredients

  • Curacha (spanner crabs) or regular mud crabs, cleaned and halved

    • For the Alavar-style Sauce:

  • Coconut milk (gata)
  • Coconut cream (kakang gata)
  • Crab paste (aligue)
  • Garlic, minced
  • Onion, minced
  • Ginger, grated
  • Turmeric powder
  • Chilies, minced
  • Brown sugar, salt, and pepper
  • Cooking oil

Make it

Recipe

  1. Steam the Curacha or crabs until fully cooked (they are naturally red even when raw, but will turn a deeper shade when cooked). Set aside.
  2. In a wide pan or wok, heat oil and sauté the garlic, onion, and ginger until fragrant.
  3. Add the shredded meat and stir-fry for a few minutes.
  4. Stir in the crab paste (aligue) and cook for 2 minutes to release its rich flavor.
  5. Pour in the coconut milk (gata). Add the turmeric powder and chilies.
  6. Bring to a gentle boil, then lower the heat and simmer for about 15 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  7. Add the thick coconut cream (kakang gata) and a pinch of brown sugar. Season with salt and pepper.
  8. Continue to simmer until the sauce reduces, thickens, and the coconut oil begins to separate slightly from the cream.
  9. Toss the steamed crabs into the sauce, coating them completely. Cook for another 3 to 5 minutes so the crabs absorb the flavors.
  10. Serve hot with a massive serving of steamed rice to mop up the extra sauce.
